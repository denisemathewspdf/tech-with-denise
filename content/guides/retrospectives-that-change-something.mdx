# Retrospectives That Actually Change Something

Most teams have had this retro: everyone says the same things they said last time ("too many meetings," "unclear requirements"), someone writes them on sticky notes, and nothing changes.

A good retro is different. It ends with **one or two small changes the team actually tries.**

## What a Retro Is

The **sprint retrospective** is a meeting at the end of a sprint where the team talks about **how it worked**, not what it built. (That's the sprint review.) The question is: "How do we get better?"

## A Simple Format: Start / Stop / Continue

Draw three columns:

- **Start:** A new habit worth trying. ("Pair up on tricky stories.")
- **Stop:** Something that hurt the team. ("Taking on more points than our velocity.")
- **Continue:** Something that's working. ("Short, focused standups.")

Everyone adds notes, the team groups similar ones, and then you talk.

## The Safety Rule

A retro only works if people feel safe being honest. The classic rule: **we all did the best we could with what we knew at the time.** Focus on the process, never on blaming a person.

## The Part Most Teams Skip: Pick One Thing

After the discussion, **vote on one or two changes** to try next sprint. Then:

- Make them **specific.** ("Ask the owner for feedback by day 5," not "communicate more.")
- Give each one an **owner.**
- **Put it on the sprint board** like any other task.
- At the next retro, start by checking whether you did it.

## Keep It Fresh

If every retro feels the same, change the format. Try:

- **Mad / Sad / Glad:** What made us frustrated, disappointed, and happy?
- **A timeline:** Draw the sprint and mark highs and lows.
- **A one-word check-in:** Everyone starts with a word that describes the sprint.

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping retros** when you're busy. That's when you need them most.
- **Letting one person dominate.** Use silent writing first.
- **Making ten action items.** You'll do none of them. Pick one or two.
- **Only focusing on the bad.** Celebrate what worked.

<Callout variant="tip">
A retro that ends with "we should communicate better" has failed. A retro that ends with "we'll hold a 10-minute check-in on Wednesday" has succeeded.
