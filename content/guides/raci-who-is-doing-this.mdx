# RACI: Who's Actually Doing This?

Picture a group project. Everyone agrees the slides need to get done. Everyone nods. And then, the night before, nobody has made the slides, because each person assumed someone else was doing them.

"I thought you were handling it."

If you've ever heard (or said) that sentence, you need a RACI chart.

## What Does RACI Stand For?

RACI is just four roles, and every task gets people assigned to them:

- **R is for Responsible.** The person (or people) who actually does the work. Hands on the keyboard.
- **A is for Accountable.** The one person who owns the outcome and has the final say. The buck stops here.
- **C is for Consulted.** People you ask for input before decisions are made. A two-way conversation.
- **I is for Informed.** People who just need to hear what's happening. A one-way update.

## The Golden Rules

1. **Every task needs exactly one Accountable person.** Two accountable people means nobody is.
2. **Every task needs at least one Responsible person.** Someone has to do it!
3. **Don't over-consult.** If you ask ten people for input on everything, nothing ever finishes.
4. **Be generous with Informed.** It costs almost nothing to keep people in the loop.

It's totally fine for one person to be both Responsible and Accountable. On solo projects, that's usually you.

## An Example You Can Steal

Say you're launching a coloring book. Here's how the roles might look:

- **Draw the illustrations:** You are Responsible.
- **Final say on the cover:** You are Accountable.
- **Proofread the pages:** Your friend Sam is Consulted.
- **Hear about the launch date:** Your Instagram followers are Informed.

Now nobody has to guess who's doing what, and Sam knows exactly how involved they're expected to be (spoiler: not too much).

## How to Make a RACI Chart

Open any spreadsheet:

1. List your **tasks** down the left side.
2. List the **people** across the top.
3. In each cell, write **R, A, C, I**, or leave it blank.
4. Check each row: exactly one A, at least one R.

Five minutes of this saves hours of "wait, whose job was this?"

<Callout variant="tip">
A RACI chart also works for tiny, non-work things. Planning a family dinner? "Who's bringing the dessert?" is a RACI question in disguise.
</Callout>

## Why It Feels So Good

Because it turns awkward assumptions into clear agreements. Nobody has to be bossy. The chart is the boss.
