# What Even Is Product Management?

Imagine you run a lemonade stand. Someone has to decide: Should we sell lemonade, or iced tea? Who's buying? What price? Do we add a cookie? How will we know if it's working?

Whoever makes those decisions is doing **product management**.

## Project Manager vs Product Manager

These two jobs sound the same and aren't:

- A **project manager** makes sure a thing gets done: on time, in order, with the right people. ("How and when?")
- A **product manager** decides what the thing should be, and why it's worth making. ("What and why?")

You can't have a perfectly run project that builds the wrong thing. That's the problem product managers exist to prevent.

## What a Product Manager Actually Does

A PM spends their time on a loop:

1. **Discover**: Talk to real people and find a problem worth solving.
2. **Define**: Decide the smallest version that would help, and how you'll measure success.
3. **Build**: Work with designers and engineers to make it real.
4. **Launch and learn**: Release it, look at what happened, and decide what to do next.

Then it starts over. That's the whole job.

## The Big Idea: Start With the Problem

Beginners often start with a solution ("let's build an app!"). PMs start with a **problem** ("first-time plant owners forget to water their plants, and the plants die"). The solution comes later, and sometimes it's not an app at all.

## What a PM Is NOT

- **Not the boss.** PMs rarely have authority over the team. They lead by being clear and making good arguments.
- **Not the one who writes the code.** (Although many learn some.)
- **Not the idea machine.** Great PMs spend more time listening than pitching.

## Words You'll Hear (Translated)

- **Product**: Anything people use to get a job done: an app, a service, even a coffee subscription.
- **MVP**: The smallest version that lets you learn something real.
- **Roadmap**: A plan showing what you'll work on Now, Next, and Later.
- **Metric**: A number that tells you whether it's working.
