# Scrum in 10 Minutes

**Scrum** is the most popular way to do agile. (The name comes from rugby, where a team huddles together to restart play.) It has a few roles, a few meetings, and one repeating loop.

## The Loop: The Sprint

Everything happens in **sprints**: short, fixed work cycles, usually one to four weeks. At the end of each sprint, the team has something finished and working. Then the next sprint begins.

## The Three Roles

- **Product Owner:** Decides what's most important to build. They own the **product backlog**, the ordered to-do list.
- **Scrum Master:** Helps the team use Scrum well and clears obstacles. Think coach, not boss.
- **Developers:** The people who build, test, and deliver the work. (This includes designers, testers, and anyone doing the work, not just coders.)

Outside the team are **stakeholders**: customers, managers, and others who give feedback.

## The Four Meetings (Called "Events")

1. **Sprint planning:** The team chooses what to build this sprint and sets a **sprint goal.**
2. **Daily scrum (standup):** 15 minutes a day. What did I do? What will I do? What's in my way?
3. **Sprint review:** At the end, the team shows the working product to stakeholders and gets feedback.
4. **Retrospective:** The team talks about how to work better next time.

## The Three Things (Called "Artifacts")

- **Product backlog:** Everything the product might need, in priority order.
- **Sprint backlog:** The stories the team chose for this sprint.
- **Increment:** The working, finished product at the end of the sprint.

## The Magic Word: Done

A story isn't done when someone says it's done. It's done when it meets the team's **Definition of Done**, a shared checklist (tested, reviewed, working in the shared environment).

## How Much to Take On?

Teams measure the work in **story points** (a relative size, not hours) and track **velocity**: how many points they finish per sprint on average. That number tells them how much to plan next time.

## A Week in a Sprint

- Day 1: Sprint planning
- Every day: Daily scrum
- Last day: Sprint review, then retrospective
- Right after: Next sprint planning

<Callout variant="tip">
Scrum is simple to understand and hard to do well. The hard part is honesty: admitting when work isn't done and when the plan was too big.
