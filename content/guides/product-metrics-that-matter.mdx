# Product Metrics That Actually Matter

You launch your app. Ten thousand downloads in a week! Champagne! 🍾

Then you check: nine thousand people opened it once and never came back.

Welcome to the difference between **vanity metrics** and **real metrics.**

## Vanity Metrics

These numbers go up easily and look impressive, but don't prove anyone got value:

- Total downloads
- Page views
- Social media followers
- Total signups ever

They're not useless, but they can't tell you if your product works.

## Actionable Metrics

These tell you whether people actually found value, and what to do next:

- **Retention:** What percent of people come back after a week? A month?
- **Activation:** What percent do the key first action (like saving their first recipe)?
- **Conversion:** What percent go from visitor to customer?
- **Churn:** What percent leave (cancel) each month?

If you can only pick one for a new product, pick **retention.** People don't keep coming back to things that don't help them.

## Write a Good Success Metric

A good one is:

- **Specific:** "40% of new users still log a watering after 4 weeks."
- **Tied to your problem:** If the problem is forgetting to water plants, success is people actually watering.
- **Time-bound:** "In the first 3 months."
- **Something you can change:** You can influence it with your product.

## Outputs vs Outcomes

An **output** is something you did or measured, like "taps on the new button went up." An **outcome** is the change in people's behavior you actually wanted, like "more people come back next week."

It's easy to celebrate outputs. Great PMs check the outcome.

## When the Numbers Look Bad

Numbers tell you **what** is happening, never **why.** If retention is low, don't guess. Talk to people who left, and look at where they dropped off. Then decide what to try.

<Callout variant="tip">
Before you launch, set up your analytics. You can't measure the first week of data you didn't collect.
</Callout>
