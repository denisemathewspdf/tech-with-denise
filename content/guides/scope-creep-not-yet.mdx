# Scope Creep and the Art of "Not Yet."

You're halfway through your project. Everything is on track. Then a friend says it:

*"You know what would be so cute? If you also added..."*

And just like that, the project grows. Then it grows again. Then it's three months later and you're still "almost done."

That's **scope creep**: when extra stuff sneaks into a project after the plan is already made, without anyone adjusting the time, money, or energy to match.

## First, Know What "Scope" Means

Scope is the list of what's **in** the project and what's **out**. A coloring book with 30 botanical illustrations, a cover, and an Amazon listing? That's the scope. A hardcover edition, an Etsy shop, and a Spanish translation? Not this time.

Writing it down at the start is the single best defense against creep. If it's not on the list, it's not in the project. (Yet.)

## Why Good Ideas Are the Dangerous Ones

Bad ideas are easy to say no to. Scope creep usually shows up as a **good** idea, from someone you like, at a moment when you're feeling optimistic. That's what makes it sneaky.

Every addition costs something: more time, more energy, and more things that could go wrong. Do it ten times and the deadline is gone.

## The Magic Phrase: "Not Yet"

You don't have to say no. You say **"Not yet."**

- *"Love that. Let's put it in the parking lot for version 2."*
- *"Great idea! It's not in scope for this launch, but I'm writing it down."*
- *"If we add that, something else has to move. What should we trade?"*

The **parking lot** is just a list where good ideas wait their turn. It's powerful because people feel heard, and your project stays safe.

## What to Do When a Change Really Is Needed

Sometimes a new request is truly important. That's fine! Real project managers handle it with a **change request**:

1. **Write down** what's being asked.
2. **Check the cost**: how much time, effort, or money does it add?
3. **Decide together**: Is it worth it? What gets cut or delayed to make room?
4. **Update the plan** so everyone sees the new version.

The key is that it's a conscious decision, not a quiet slide.

<Callout variant="tip">
A quick test for any new idea: "If we add this, what happens to the deadline?" If the answer is "nothing, somehow," be suspicious.
</Callout>

## Scope Creep Isn't Just for Work

It happens when you plan a party ("let's add a magician!"), move apartments ("while we're at it, let's repaint!"), and build apps ("what if we add a leaderboard?"). Same sneaky pattern, same fix.
