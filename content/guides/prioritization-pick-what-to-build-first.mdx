# Prioritization: How to Pick What to Build First

You have 30 ideas, a tiny team, and everyone is sure THEIR idea is the most important. Welcome to product management.

You can't build everything, so you need a fair way to choose. Here are two simple ones.

## Method 1: Impact vs Effort

Draw a square with two lines through it:

- **Up** is bigger impact. **Right** is more effort.
- **High impact, low effort** (do first): quick wins.
- **High impact, high effort** (plan carefully): big bets.
- **Low impact, low effort** (maybe later): nice-to-haves.
- **Low impact, high effort** (skip): time sinks.

It takes five minutes and gets a team talking about the same picture.

## Method 2: RICE Scoring

When you want numbers, use **RICE**:

- **R**each: How many people will this affect (in a set time)?
- **I**mpact: How much will it move the needle for each person? (Often scored 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, or 3.)
- **C**onfidence: How sure are you? (As a percent: 100%, 80%, 50%.)
- **E**ffort: How much work, in person-weeks?

**Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort**

For example: Reach 500, Impact 2, Confidence 80%, Effort 4 weeks. That's 500 × 2 × 0.8 ÷ 4 = **200.**

Compare that to another idea and the higher score usually goes first.

## Be Honest About Confidence

Confidence is the secret ingredient. If you're guessing, say 50%. It makes shaky ideas rank lower until you've tested them, which is exactly right.

## Use Scores as a Conversation Starter

RICE doesn't make the decision. It makes the **argument clearer.** If someone disagrees with a score, you get to ask, "Which number do you think is wrong?" That's a much better conversation than "my idea is better."

## What Prioritizing Is Really About

It's mostly about **saying no.** Every yes to one thing is a no to something else. Good PMs say "not yet" a lot, and explain why.

<Callout variant="tip">
Always tie your priorities back to your goal. If an idea scores well but doesn't move your success metric, ask why.
