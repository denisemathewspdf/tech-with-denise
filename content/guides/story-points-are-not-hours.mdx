# Story Points Are Not Hours

"How long will it take?" is one of the hardest questions in work, because the honest answer is "it depends." On who does it. On how tired they are. On what goes wrong.

That's why many agile teams don't answer in hours. They use **story points.**

## What a Story Point Is

A story point is a **relative size.** It describes how big something is compared to other things the team has already done, taking into account effort, complexity, and uncertainty.

It's not time. A 5-point story doesn't mean "five hours" or "five days."

## Why Not Just Use Hours?

- People are terrible at predicting hours. We're much better at comparing: "This is about twice as hard as that."
- Hours change from person to person. Points are the team's shared measure.
- Hours feel like promises. Points feel like estimates (which they are).

## How to Do It

1. **Pick a reference story** the team understands, like "Choose toppings" = 3 points.
2. **Compare new stories to it.** "Pay by card" feels about twice as hard? That's 6 points.
3. **Use a simple scale.** Many teams use 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, a version of the Fibonacci sequence. The gaps grow because bigger work is more uncertain, so you don't pretend to be precise.
4. **Talk it out.** If one person says 3 and another says 13, the conversation matters more than the number. Someone knows something the other doesn't.

## What They're For: Velocity

Add up the points the team **finishes** in each sprint and average them. That's the team's **velocity.** If it's usually about 20, plan about 20 points next sprint. That's how Scrum teams decide how much to take on.

## Common Mistakes

- **Converting points to hours.** It defeats the purpose.
- **Comparing teams.** One team's 5 is another team's 8. That's fine.
- **Using velocity as a performance score.** The moment you do, people inflate the points.
- **Estimating huge stories.** If a story is 13 or bigger, try splitting it.
