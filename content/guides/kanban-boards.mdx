# Kanban Boards: Sticky Notes With Ambition

Imagine a whiteboard with three columns: **To Do**, **Doing**, and **Done**. Every task is a sticky note. As work happens, you move the notes from left to right.

That's it. That's a kanban board. And somehow this humble pile of sticky notes runs huge software teams, kitchens, and hospitals.

## Where the Weird Name Comes From

"Kanban" is Japanese for roughly "visual signal." It started in a Toyota factory, where workers used cards to show what needed to be made next. Same idea, nicer sticky notes.

## Why It Works So Well

A big project in your head is a blurry cloud. A kanban board turns it into something you can see at a glance:

- **What's waiting** (To Do)
- **What's happening right now** (Doing)
- **What's finished** (Done)

You instantly know where things stand, and so does everyone else. No more "wait, did anyone start the cover?" You can literally see it.

## How to Build One in 2 Minutes

1. **Write every task on its own card.** One task per card, small enough to finish in a day or two.
2. **Put them all in To Do.**
3. **Pull one into Doing** when you actually start it.
4. **Move it to Done** when it's really done (not "almost done").
5. **Repeat** until the To Do column is empty. 🎉

You can use real sticky notes, or free tools like Trello, Notion, or GitHub Projects. The tool doesn't matter. The columns do.

## The Secret Rule: Don't Do Everything at Once

Here's the sneaky trick that makes kanban more than a to-do list: **limit how many things can be in Doing at the same time.** Many teams call this a work-in-progress limit, or WIP limit.

Why? Because starting ten things and finishing none feels busy but gets nothing shipped. If your Doing column has three cards max, you have to finish something before you can start something new. Focus is a feature.

<Callout variant="tip">
If your Doing column is overflowing, that's the board trying to tell you something. Either you're juggling too much, or something is stuck. Find the stuck card first.
</Callout>

## Make It Yours

Three columns is the classic. But you can add more as your project needs them:

- **Backlog** for ideas you're parking for later
- **Review** for things waiting on someone's feedback
- **Blocked** for cards that can't move until something else happens

Start simple. Add columns only when you feel the need.

## Quick Example

Say you're launching a coloring book. Mid-project, your board might show "Sketch illustrations" in Done, "Ink illustrations" in Doing, and "Design the cover," "Format the interior," and "Upload to KDP" in To Do. One look and you know exactly where you are.
