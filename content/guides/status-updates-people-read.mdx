# Status Updates People Actually Read

A status update is a short message that tells people how your project is going. Simple, right?

And yet most status updates are either "All good! 🙂" (which tells nobody anything) or a giant wall of text nobody reads past line two.

Here's a better way. It takes about two minutes, and it makes you look like someone who has it together.

## The Three-Sentence Formula

Great updates answer three questions:

1. **Where are we?** (The status.)
2. **What's in the way?** (The problem, if there is one.)
3. **What are you doing about it?** (The plan.)

That's it. Three sentences, tops.

## Meet RAG Status

Lots of teams start the update with a color, called **RAG status** (Red, Amber/Yellow, Green):

- 🟢 **Green:** On track. No worries.
- 🟡 **Yellow:** Something's slipping, but I have a plan to fix it.
- 🔴 **Red:** We're off track and I need help or a decision.

The color gives people the headline in half a second. Then your sentences give them the details.

## Before and After

**The "please don't read this" version:**
*"I'm behind and stressed and I don't know what to do."*

**The "all good!" version (which isn't helpful either):**
*"All good! 🙂"*

**The version people love:**
*"🟡 Yellow: Inking is one week behind. I'm adding two extra hours on Saturdays, so we're still on track for the March 1 launch."*

See the difference? You know where things stand, what the issue is, and what happens next. Nobody has to ask a follow-up question.

## Tips for Updates That Get Read

- **Lead with the headline.** The color or the main point goes first.
- **Be honest early.** A yellow update this week is much easier to hear than a surprise red next month.
- **Pair every problem with a plan.** Even "I need two days to figure out options" counts.
- **Keep a rhythm.** Same time, same place, every week. Predictable updates build trust.
- **Ask for what you need.** If you need a decision or help, say so in plain words.

<Callout variant="tip">
Never hide bad news. People can work with "we're a week behind." They can't work with "wait, why didn't anyone tell me?"
</Callout>

## Your Template

Copy and paste this:

> **[🟢/🟡/🔴] [Short headline].** [One sentence on where things stand.] [One sentence on the problem and what you're doing about it.]

Fill it in, hit send, and enjoy being the most organized person in the chat.
